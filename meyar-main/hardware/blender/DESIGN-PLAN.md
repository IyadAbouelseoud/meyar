# F1 industrial design plan — Meyar hardware tiers

> Research behind the F1 renders (form, materials, cost, ease of use), compiled 21 Nov 2026 from primary sources where they could be opened. Every number below carries its citation inline. **None of these numbers has a row in `docs/sources.md` yet**, so none may go on a slide, poster or UI until the source owner adds the row (AGENTS.md §8). Figures marked as search-snippet or unverified need the page opened first. T3 and T4 are "Concept render — not built".


## Summary: build the kit first, render the rest

Meyar should treat its three hardware tiers very differently. **T1, the mail-back saliva kit, can be built now from generic parts.** It needs a screw-cap tube of about 2 mL with a separate funnel, a pre-applied Code 128 label running along the tube axis, a leakproof bag with absorbent, and a rigid mailer that meets the IATA PI650 physical rules while being marked "Exempt human specimen". Its biggest unknowns are regulatory and logistical, not form: whether Saudi Post will accept domestic specimen mail, and whether any buffer stays stable in Gulf summer heat. **T3, the cath-lab CYP2C19 cartridge unit, is a credible concept.** It improves on the cleared devices (Genomadix Cube, Spartan RX) mainly by putting the screen inside the device and binding the cartridge to the patient ID. It must be labelled "Concept render — not built", and it must not claim an outcome benefit: TAILOR-PCI missed its primary endpoint (HR 0.66, 95% CI 0.43–1.02, P=.06). **T4, the multi-gene reader, goes beyond anything a regulator has cleared.** The notes found no cleared multi-gene near-patient PGx device. The render is honest only if it shows separate reaction chambers, no results, and CYP2D6 explicitly marked as not assessed, because SNP chambers cannot measure copy number. Very few cost figures are sourced. There is one distributor list price for a research saliva kit, one unverified NICE figure for the CYP2C19 platform, and one NICE per-test price for a different single-gene kit. Every Meyar-specific unit cost is a "no sourced figure".

## What the renders show and why

The hackathon renders are design statements, and each visible feature has a reason. The table below ties each one to its evidence or to the constraint it respects.

## T1 can ship as a generic, patent-aware triple package

**Form.** The functional core is the generic architecture described in FDA's Oragene•Dx decision summary: a collection tube with a funnel, stabilising liquid that mixes with the saliva, and "a small cap... to close the tube for transport and storage", after which the funnel is thrown away ([FDA K192920](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/reviews/K192920.pdf)). **About 2 mL of saliva** is the common sample target. DNA Genotek lists 2 mL for OG-500 ([DNA Genotek OG-500](https://dnagenotek.com/ROW/products/collection-human/oragene-dna/500-series/OG-500)), and the Oragene•Dx usability acceptance window is **1–4 mL total sample volume** ([DNA Genotek OGD-500](https://www.dnagenotek.com/US/products/collection-human/oragene-dx/500-series/OGD-500)). Meyar should keep the sample-to-buffer ratio fixed, as K192920 says its models do, and make the fill line very visible. The protectable part of the category is the mechanism that releases the buffer as the lid closes, not the tube itself. For that reason the separate funnel and plain screw cap in the render are the right default. Meyar has three ways to add buffer that avoid copying the patented mechanisms: a pre-filled tube with buffer at the bottom, a separate ampoule the user pours in, or a mechanism Meyar designs itself. Each still needs a freedom-to-operate search in Saudi Arabia, the GCC and the country of manufacture. The notes did not check whether any listed patent is in force, lapsed or expired in Saudi Arabia. The hinged funnel lid of the Oragene product is a brand-specific look and should not be copied.

**Materials.** The notes found **no sourced polymer, wall thickness, tube dimensions or cap torque** for any saliva tube. Polypropylene is the usual assumption, but Meyar has to get it from a vendor certificate of conformity. Label stock is better documented. Options include Zebra 8000T CryoCool thermal-transfer polypropylene with a cold-temperature acrylic adhesive rated to **−196 °C** ([Zebra spec sheet](https://www.zebra.com/content/dam/zebra_dam/en/spec-sheets/8000t-cryocool-spec-sheet-en-us.pdf)); Brady cryogenic polypropylene labels rated **−80 °C to 70 °C** ([Fisher Scientific](https://www.fishersci.com/shop/products/chemical-resistant-cryogenic-polypropylene-laboratory-labels-vial-top-m6-m7-printers/17004901)); and self-laminating polyester labels rated **−196 to +130 °C** that resist ethanol, isopropanol and 10% bleach ([Avantor](https://www.avantorsciences.com/ni/en/product/44870001/self-laminating-cryogenic-polyester-labels-for-m410-bmp41-m510-bmp51-bmp53-and-m511-printers)). A self-laminating polyester label is the most defensible choice, because the buffer may contain alcohol and labs may freeze samples. The label should leave a clear window so the fill line stays visible. CLSI AUTO12 sets a standard clinical label size of **2 × 1 in (50.8 × 25.4 mm)** ([CLSI AUTO12](https://clsi.org/shop/standards/auto12/)). AUTO02 recommends placing the label 9 mm from the bottom and 10 mm from the top of the container ([CLSI AUTO02](https://clsi.org/shop/standards/auto02/)). The quiet-zone and X-dimension values for Code 128 sit in paywalled standards: no sourced figure. Lab-facing fields should use Latin script and Western digits. Arabic belongs on the outer kit and the instructions for use (IFU).

**Packaging and shipping rules.** The design default is a single mailer that physically meets the stricter UN3373 rules but is marked for the looser category. Under WHO's 2025–2026 guidance, exempt human specimens are those with "minimal likelihood" of pathogens. Germline testing unrelated to infection fits that definition, but the shipper must use "sound, professional judgement", and if the shipper cannot conclude that the risk is minimal, the specimen becomes Category B ([WHO 2025–2026](https://iris.who.int/server/api/core/bitstreams/ade27b2c-372d-487c-b794-9899318480cb/content)). The IATA PI650 physical rules for Category B are **95 kPa** internal-pressure resistance for the primary or secondary, absorbent for the entire contents, a **1.2 m drop in any orientation**, at least one outer face of **100 × 100 mm**, and an itemised contents list between the secondary and outer packaging ([IATA DGR 67th ed. PI650](https://www.iata.org/contentassets/b08040a138dc4442a4f066e6fb99fe2a/dgr-67-en-pi650.pdf)). If Meyar builds the mailer to those rules, a later change in classification only means adding the UN3373 diamond (sides of at least 50 mm), not redesigning the mailer. PI650 also requires packaging makers to give "clear instructions on filling and closing" to the person packing the parcel, which can be the patient. So the IFU is part of compliance, not just a usability feature. DNA Genotek's vendor marketing puts saliva shipping at about **USD 5 per sample**, against about USD 80 for blood ([DNA Genotek blog](https://blog.dnagenotek.com/dna-saliva-kits-or-traditional-blood-collection-which-is-more-cost-effective)). Saudi postage: no sourced figure.

**Heat is an unsolved packaging problem.** Room-temperature stability claims differ by vendor. Oragene•Dx states **1 year** ([DNA Genotek OGD-500](https://www.dnagenotek.com/US/products/collection-human/oragene-dx/500-series/OGD-500)). PAXgene states **24 months at up to 25 °C** ([QIAGEN](https://www.qiagen.com/us/products/discovery-and-translational-research/sample-collection-stabilization/dna/paxgene-saliva-collector)), though that comes from a search snippet that was not opened. The notes found **no published stability data above 40 °C** for any saliva DNA kit. Meyar should agree a validated temperature range with its buffer vendor before it promises mail-back in summer. An insulated mailer, or an instruction not to leave the kit in a car, is a design hedge, not a proven fix.

## Arabic pictograms work only when words travel with them

The evidence that lay people can collect saliva DNA at home is strong. In the Oragene•Dx lay-user study, **206 adults** collected at home from written instructions, and **97.35%** of samples met the minimum DNA requirement, rising to 98.41% after re-extraction. The FDA review also included a Flesch-Kincaid readability analysis of the labelling ([FDA K192920](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/reviews/K192920.pdf)). That study's population was US-representative, however. Gulf and Saudi research changes how Meyar's IFU should look. In a Saudi caregiver study, adding pictograms to Arabic leaflets raised mean correct answers from **4.38 to 5.25**, and **43%** of participants had possible limited health literacy ([PMC9177450](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9177450)). A Qatar study found that "pictogram-only labels were the least comprehended" ([QU QSpace](https://qspace.qu.edu.qa/handle/10576/37294)). The plan is therefore numbered pictogram panels, each paired with one short Arabic sentence and with English as a secondary language. No step should rely on a pictogram alone. The panels should read right to left, and the sequence arrows should be mirrored. Panels that show real hand movements or thread direction should not be mirrored, because "turn to close" has to match the physical cap. That RTL rule is an inference: the notes found no study of RTL pictogram sequences for medical devices.

The likely steps are: wait before collecting (the commonly used 30-minute no-food rule comes only from an unopened university IFU snippet, so it must be checked against the vendor's IFU); spit to the fill line; add or release the buffer; remove the funnel and cap the tube; shake; bag and seal; box and post. For older users, the render should show a large ribbed transport cap, a high-contrast fill line and an assisted-collection option for dry mouth (the sponge in OGD-575 is the precedent cited in K192920). The notes found **no sourced cap-torque or grip-force figure** for older adults and no peer-reviewed study of older adults using home saliva kits. The honest route is a local lay-user study modelled on K192920, measuring return rate, sample adequacy, comprehension and a readability check of the Arabic text, run with older and low-literacy participants deliberately over-recruited.

## T3 differentiates on integration and identity binding, not on clinical claims

**What already exists.** The two FDA-cleared near-patient CYP2C19 systems, Genomadix Cube (K220026, cleared March 2023) and Spartan RX (K123891), both use buccal swabs and are sample-to-result PCR systems. Both detect only ***2, *3 and *17** ([FDA K220026](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/reviews/K220026.pdf); [FDA K123891](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/reviews/K123891.pdf)). Each is a group of separate boxes: a thermal cycler, a consumer netbook UI and a barcode scanner, and Spartan adds a printer. Genomadix replaced Spartan's frozen, allele-specific tubes (stored at −20 to −80 °C and used within 45 minutes of leaving the freezer) with one room-temperature cartridge holding three tubes. That is the trend Meyar should build on. The performance bar is high. Genomadix reached a **99% final correct-call rate (429/433)** in method comparison and **99.1%** first pass across 960 reproducibility tests. Time to result is about one hour, but that figure is vendor-reported in secondary sources ([360Dx](https://www.360dx.com/molecular-diagnostics/genomadix-gets-fda-510k-clearance-point-care-pcr-system-cyp2c19-test)), not stated in the 510(k).

**Form.** The notes found no sourced dimensions, weight or housing description for either device. Meyar should therefore not claim a size, and it should differ by architecture rather than by silhouette. The architecture is one object with an integrated screen. It has a single keyed front slot that accepts the cartridge only one way and confirms seating by touch and sound. A door locks during the run. A status light bar is readable from across a dim room. A built-in scanner binds the patient wristband to the cartridge barcode before the cartridge can be inserted. The cartridge-to-kit-ID binding in the render is the design response to the suspected sample swaps behind both incorrect Genomadix calls. One honest limit: both cleared precedents use buccal swabs, and the swab-to-port mapping is a critical task in its own right. One port per swab, shape-keyed ports, or a single port with internal splitting are all unvalidated options. The render's single slot should not suggest that sample loading has been solved.

**Materials and cleanability.** Disinfectants usually damage plastic housings through environmental stress cracking at points of moulded-in or assembly stress, not through surface attack ([Covestro case study](https://solutions.covestro.com/en/highlights/articles/cases/2021/disinfectant-influence-on-polycarbonate-based-materials)). General-purpose PC and PC/ABS are the weak spots. Covestro's chart, seen only through a search summary of a PDF that returned 403, rates Bayblend FR 3010 PC/ABS as not resistant to Oxivir Tb accelerated hydrogen peroxide, while Makrolon M6011 FR and Makroblend M5005 FR pass ([Covestro brochure](https://solutions.covestro.com/-/media/covestro/solution-center/brochures/pdf/cov-025-durable-materials-for-medical-device-housings-063021-_004__final11112021.pdf)). Eastman reports that Tritan MX711/MX731 keep **about 96–116% of impact strength** after hydrogen-peroxide and IPA-quat exposure, while PC cracked in a quat dip ([Eastman SPMBS5190](https://www.eastman.com/Literature_Center/S/SPMBS5190.pdf)). All of this is vendor data. The shortlist for the shell is Tritan MX/MXF, Makroblend M5005 FR or Makrolon M6011 FR. Meyar should strain-jig test whichever it picks against the hospital's actual disinfectant list. The shell form follows FDA's warning about hinges, crevices and mated surfaces: a one-piece upper shell with no visible fasteners, large radii, a flush screen and sealed membrane button, a self-closing slot shutter, parting lines away from the wiping surfaces, and vents only at the rear or underside.

**Ease of use and validation.** The evidence that non-laboratory staff can run such tests is solid. RAPID GENE nurses ran the Spartan RX after one **30-minute** training session ([Medical Xpress](https://medicalxpress.com/news/2012-03-world-bedside-genetic-green-lancet.amp)). In TAILOR-PCI, point-of-care results run by non-laboratory staff at 40 centres matched laboratory TaqMan genotyping in **99.1% (2,364/2,385)** of cases ([PMC7448831](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7448831)). FDA's CLIA-waiver guidance asks that instructions be no higher than a **7th-grade reading level**, with a quick-reference guide and flex studies that test user error ([FDA CLIA waiver guidance](https://fda.gov/media/109582/download)). The FDA human-factors guidance (revised 3 Aug 2026) generally expects **at least 15 participants per distinct user group** in validation ([FDA HF/UE guidance](https://www.fda.gov/media/80481/download)). Cath-lab nurses or technologists, cardiologists and POCT coordinators could each count as a separate group. Simulated-use tests should copy the cath lab: dim fluoroscopy lighting, gloves and lead aprons, interruptions, and other equipment nearby.

**Clinical framing.** TAILOR-PCI (JAMA 2020) randomised **5,302** PCI patients. In carriers of loss-of-function alleles, the primary endpoint was **4.0% vs 5.9%, HR 0.66 (95% CI 0.43–1.02), P=.06, which is not statistically significant** ([PMC7448831](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7448831); [ACC summary](https://www.acc.org/Latest-in-Cardiology/Clinical-Trials/2020/03/26/19/53/TAILOR-PCI)). A post-hoc finding of fewer early events is exploratory ([Healio](https://www.healio.com/news/cardiology/20200914/rapid-bedside-genetic-testing-enhances-safety-efficacy-of-antiplatelet-therapy)). Because the result takes about an hour, the realistic workflow is to swab at consent or prep, run during or after the case, and adjust the P2Y12 inhibitor before discharge. Meyar's T3 materials should present the unit as faster identification of the genotype, not as proven outcome benefit. The cleared device itself is labelled "not intended to be used to predict drug response or non-response".

## T4's honest render shows chambers, not answers

**The state of the art is single-gene.** The notes found **no regulator-cleared multi-gene near-patient PGx genotyping system**. This is based on targeted searches, not an exhaustive database query. Both near-patient products found test only CYP2C19: the Genomadix Cube ([FDA K220026](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf22/K220026.pdf)) and the Genedrive CYP2C19 ID Kit. Genedrive's allele count differs by source: six alleles according to NICE, "five DNA variants" according to the company. NICE recommends Genedrive only when laboratory testing is not available ([NICE DG59](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/DG59/chapter/1-recommendations)). Published isothermal PGx research reaches **2–4 SNPs** per sample, always as separate reactions. The Chen centrifugal chip covered 4 SNPs in 32 cells, but it still needed **30 cycles of PCR pre-amplification**, and a single-base mismatch gave only about a 2-fold signal difference ([PMC8179484](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8179484)). A 2026 CRISPR CYP2C19 assay reached 97.3–100% accuracy for each allele in 110 participants. It needed genotype cutoffs set "individually for each variant" and was not tested on saliva ([ACS Sensors 2026, MDC PDF](https://edoc.mdc-berlin.de/id/eprint/26643/1/26643oa.pdf)).

**Form follows spatial multiplexing.** The defensible architecture is a disposable cartridge with one chamber per SNP or allele, dry reagents, clearly separate control chambers, and at most one or two colour channels per chamber. Multiplexing comes from the number of chambers, not the number of colours. A disc roughly the size of a CD (about 8 cm) with chambers arranged radially matches academic precedent, not any commercial product's shape. It implies a spin motor, a lid interlock and a read zone, and the tinted lid in the render hints at those parts without detailing them. The notes found no sourced dimensions, weight, power or optical-channel count for any POC PGx reader, so the render must not imply a size. Sample type is an open risk. Saliva has been shown only for tube-based PE-LAMP with 2 SNPs ([Theranostics 2019](https://thno.org/v09p3723.htm)), so "spit in, multi-gene answer out" goes beyond the evidence. T4 shares the T3 housing logic and should use the same disinfectant-resistant shell materials. That carry-over is an inference, not separately sourced.

**CYP2D6 is an absolute exclusion.** CYP2D6 phenotype depends on whole-gene deletion (*5), duplications and CYP2D6/2D7 hybrids. PharmCAT states that ultrarapid metabolizers "cannot be called using only SNPs and INDELs", and that hemizygous variants "will be falsely presented as homozygous" ([PharmCAT](https://pharmcat.clinpgx.org/using/Calling-CYP2D6)). AMP/CAP guidance says clinical CYP2D6 testing combines genotype with copy-number results at several gene regions ([J Mol Diagn 2021, PMC8579245](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8579245)). A SNP chamber measures relative allele signal, not gene dosage. The hatched "Not assessed — copy number" slot is therefore the only honest way to show CYP2D6, and T4 must never be shown answering it. The same caution covers phenotype labels such as "ultrarapid". Gene slots should be limited to SNP-defined targets: CYP2C19 has cleared near-patient precedent, and CYP2C9 and CYP1A1 have only research isothermal precedent. Showing no results at all keeps the render clear of the cleared device's own limit, which is genotype calls with inconclusive and retest states and no drug-response claims. Genomadix's first pass had about 4% inconclusive calls ([FDA K220026](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf22/K220026.pdf)), and NICE's base case assumed an **8% test-failure rate** for point-of-care testing ([NICE DG59 DAR addendum 3](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/DG59/documents/d-a-r-addendum-3)). A future T4 UI would need a retest path.

## Conclusion

The three tiers are not one product line at three levels of maturity. Each faces a different kind of risk. T1's risk is logistical and regulatory: domestic Saudi Post acceptance, buffer stability above 40 °C, and freedom to operate on buffer release. None of these is solved by better form, so the next step is letters and quotes, not CAD. T3's risk lies in human factors and identity: its best feature is the cartridge-to-patient binding, a direct answer to the documented failure of the predicate devices, and that is what should be prototyped and tested with 15 or more users per group. T4's risk is scientific: the render is useful precisely because it shows where near-patient genotyping stops, at copy number. Showing that limit openly is a credibility asset for Meyar in front of clinical judges.

One link connects all three. The Code 128 kit ID printed on T1 (MYR-DEMO-0001 in the demo) is the same identifier the T3 cartridge binds to. Meyar's lasting design asset is therefore a chain-of-custody identity system that runs from the mail-back tube to the bedside cartridge. Later readers can use that identity system once they exist.
